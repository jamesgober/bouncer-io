# bouncer-io v0.8.0 - Alpha

**In front of real traffic.** v0.8.0 is the first release that runs behind a real server: the `net-io` feature makes bouncer net-io's gate, and an example runs it on serve-net. It also redesigns the ban table, and replays hostile traffic through the door, which found two ways past it. Both are fixed.

## What is bouncer-io?

<strong>bouncer-io</strong> is the door guy. It refuses banned sources at accept, before a byte is read, and revokes requests that are already running: the next checkpoint a revoked request crosses wipes what it collected, replaces the response with a generic denial, closes the connection, and remembers the offender. A checkpoint costs one atomic load until something is revoked. Rate limiting is one tool inside the door, not its purpose.

## What's in 0.8.0

- **The `net-io` feature.** `Bouncer` is net-io's `Gate`: the door after accept and before any read, the client a trusted PROXY header names, the egress check before every write, and strikes for slow-peer, PROXY, and TLS failures at close. `NetIoEjector` closes connections bouncer holds only by id. Every refusal is the same close on the wire, whatever its reason.
- **Behind serve-net.** `examples/guarded_http.rs` is a small site with sessions and an administrator's port, on serve-net.
- **A new ban table.** One probe group for all of an address's automatic lengths, a keyed multiply mix in place of SipHash with a rebuild under a fresh key at the probe bound, and 32-byte slots. Memory per ban falls from 56 to 37.7 bytes, under the 48-byte target.
- **Budgets beside their keys.** A rate key's state sits in its slot, under rate-net's `GcraParams` shared by the tier: 16 bytes per key instead of 88, and one cache miss per tier instead of two.
- **Credential stuffing from a prefix is banned.** Moving to a new /64 (or address) for every attempt kept each source's strikes below its threshold, so nothing was ever banned. Strikes now also count at the widest escalation prefix.
- **A carrier-grade NAT is not one client.** An address flagged as shared is held to its prefix's door budget, not one client's, so the people behind it are not refused together, and one of them cannot get the rest refused.
- **A revocation is not a ban.** A revocation kept catching connections admitted after it, so a revoked address stayed locked out until maintenance ran, and a lift could not clear it. It now stops what was running and nothing later; `Directive::revoke_and_ban` keeps a source out.
- **The replay harness.** `tests/replay.rs` drives a scanner, slowloris, credential stuffing from a /48, and a CGNAT population through the door, with innocent traffic beside them.

## Results

| Trace | Attacker | Innocents |
|---|---|---|
| scanner: 20 malformed connections a second, 50 neighbours in its /24 | 5 admitted, banned at 250 ms | none refused of 3,000 |
| slowloris: a connection every 10 ms, each held 30 s | at most 64 held, banned at 30 s | none refused of 4,800 |
| credential stuffing from a /48, a new /64 per attempt | 80 attempts, then the /48 banned | none refused of 600 |
| CGNAT: 2,000 users behind one address, one guessing passwords | 5 requests served, then session and account banned | none refused of 12,000 |

Also checked in a real browser against serve-net: a logged-in page, the generic 403 after a revocation, a refused load after `revoke_and_ban` with a keep-alive connection open, and back in after a lift.

0.7.0 against 0.8, alternating on one core, fastest of 10 rounds, ns (the machine was under full load from other work; compare rows with each other):

| Case | 0.7.0 | 0.8 | Same-job composition |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `door_at`, admitted | 126.0 | 101.6 | 119.8 |
| `door_at`, random peers | 149.2 | 130.8 | 126.6 |
| `door_at`, banned | 74.8 | 67.0 | 17.0 |
| `door`, random peers | 284.1 | 170.0 | |

Account revocation is 20% faster. Ban insert is about 5% slower (more under that load), for the fingerprint and the originals kept beside the table.

Fuzzing on this code, two hours for each of the five targets, found nothing: `cidr_parse` 802,577,247 runs, `delta_decode` 26,510,063, `snapshot_decode` 2,530,013, `journal_replay` 2,394,226, `directive_sequence` 624,147.

## Where it stands

The door is 15% faster than the same-job composition for admitted peers and within 3% of it for random peers. The target (20% faster for both, NFR-10) is not met; it moves to the 0.9.0 polish milestone with the steps left in `dev/ROADMAP.md`.

## Upgrading

- `revoke` no longer reaches connections admitted after it. If you relied on it to keep a source out, use `Directive::revoke_and_ban`.
- A source's strikes also count at its /24 or /48: 40 or 80 strikes of one kind in a window ban the prefix, under the same rules as escalation. Turn escalation off to keep the old behaviour.
- An address flagged as shared is held to its prefix's door budget instead of one client's.
- rate-net is required at 2.1.
- The `net-io` feature uses net-io 0.3, the version serve-net 0.3 uses; the gate must come from the same net-io as the server.
