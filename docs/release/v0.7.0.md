# bouncer-io v0.7.0 - Hardening

**Attacked on purpose, fixed test-first.** v0.7.0 is the hardening release: every threat in the plan has its named test, every edge case of the plan is tested, and the defects that turned up on the way are fixed. The largest is sharing: nodes now agree on every ban after any order and repetition of delivery, which they did not before.

## What is bouncer-io?

<strong>bouncer-io</strong> is the door guy. It refuses banned sources at accept, before a byte is read, and revokes requests that are already running: the next checkpoint a revoked request crosses wipes what it collected, replaces the response with a generic denial, closes the connection, and remembers the offender. A checkpoint costs one atomic load until something is revoked. Rate limiting is one tool inside the door, not its purpose.

## What's in 0.7.0

- **Sharing converges.** Each node's ban on a key is kept apart with its stamp, and lifts are scoped: to their own node's bans, or to every node's for an administrator's lift delivered through the administration channel. A node still cannot lift another node's bans. A three-node simulation with partitions, a clock 30 seconds ahead, reordering, and duplication agrees on every ban over 5,000 seeds. Records are format 2; format-1 files and deltas are read, and a format-1 snapshot is rewritten on load.
- **Forged UDP cannot frame a prefix.** Unverified datagrams were charged to the same prefix budget as that prefix's connections, so forged traffic could get a victim's TCP connections refused. They now have a budget key of their own.
- **Full tables are never unlimited.** Once both rate tables were full, newcomers passed uncharged; they now share one global budget at the prefix rate. Concurrency tables hold every live connection, and `Health::budget_load` no longer reports a healthy bouncer as unhealthy.
- **Enforcement holds when memory is full.** An enforced strike whose ban does not fit still revokes the peer (`StrikeOutcome::RevokedNotBanned`).
- **Events under a flood are sampled**, as documented: at most 100 a second per sampled signal, each with the count it stands for, so a slow sink cannot slow the refusal path.
- **Smaller fixes:** a stronger later revocation raises a denied ticket's code; a proxied request that never binds its client is reported after the handshake deadline; a connection closed under live requests reports them revoked; the epoch steps over the value that marks always-checking tickets.
- **Tests:** timing tests for T-8 (Kolmogorov-Smirnov, with a control that must separate), the 24-hour soak, the full T-6 run on 32 threads, the three-node simulation, and the missing threat and edge-case tests. The decoder fuzz targets now reach the record decoders behind the checksums.
- **Docs:** `docs/SECURITY.md` (trust boundaries, threats, defences, costs, how to report a vulnerability) and `docs/PLATFORM-NOTES.md`.

## Results

- **Fuzzing:** the five decoder and sequence targets ran 24 hours each on the final 0.7 code with no findings: `cidr_parse` 22.1 billion runs, `delta_decode` 1.0 billion, `snapshot_decode` 79.9 million, `journal_replay` 77.7 million, `directive_sequence` 15.1 million.
- **Soak (NFR-13):** 24 hours at 93,750 connections a second on the final 0.7 code: 540 million connections, 57.1 million bans, 5.0 million lifts, 57.1 million revocations, no missed revocation. Live heap from the first hour to the last stayed at 45.98 MB, varying by under 6 KB.
- **Sharing:** the three-node simulation agrees on every ban over 5,000 seeds.
- **Timing (T-8):** admitted latency next to banned neighbours and bind latency near banned digests are not separable; the control that must separate does.

## Upgrading

- Snapshots, journals, and deltas are format 2. A 0.7 node reads 0.5 and 0.6 files and deltas; 0.5 and 0.6 refuse 0.7's with `UnsupportedFormat`. Upgrade every node of a cluster before relying on deltas between them.
- `import` needs a node id, as `export_delta` did.
- On a node that shares, a lift by an automated actor removes only that node's own ban; deliver an administrator's lift through the administration channel to lift a key on every node.
