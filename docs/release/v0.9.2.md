# bouncer-io v0.9.2 - Filter and anchor

**A faster door, persistence rollback detected, and every loom model checked again.** Most peers with no ban at any of their lengths are now cleared by a filter that stays in cache, so the door no longer reads the ban table for them. A store put back from an older backup is refused at load when a version anchor is kept outside it. Writing a new loom model showed that most of the existing ones never ran the other thread first; they now explore every schedule. Upgrade from 0.9.1; the API only gains.

## What is bouncer-io?

<strong>bouncer-io</strong> is the door guy. It refuses banned sources at accept, before a byte is read, and revokes requests that are already running: the next checkpoint a revoked request crosses wipes what it collected, replaces the response with a generic denial, closes the connection, and remembers the offender. A checkpoint costs one atomic load until something is revoked. Rate limiting is one tool inside the door, not its purpose.

## The door

- **A filter in front of the ban table.** Each address key sets four bits in the 64-byte block of its anchor prefix, so one block answers for every automatic length of an address. A "no" is exact. The table is probed only for the lengths the filter cannot rule out. Lifts and expiries leave stale bits, which are cleared by rebuilding the second buffer once they reach a third of the keys.
- **Administrator-covered peers are refused without a hash**, from a reference computed when the administrator set is built, and without probing the automatic lengths unless one may hold a ban. Which ban answers is unchanged.

0.9.1 against 0.9.2, alternating on one pinned core, with the machine busy with other work (relative only):

| Case | change |
|---|---:|
| `door_at`, admitted | -11.6% |
| `door_at`, random peers | -19.0% |
| `door_at`, random peers near the bans | -21.2% |
| `door_at`, banned | +7.8% |
| judgement, 1,000,000 bans and the administrator set, random | -32.4% |

Banned peers pay the filter before the probe. That is the trade, and the instruction gate's baseline records it. With cold peers, one call at a time, an admitted peer's median goes from 254 to 297 ns down to 180 to 191 ns, and a banned peer's from 296 to 351 ns up to 466 to 477 ns, because a cold banned peer now misses the cache twice. Memory per ban goes from 37.7 to 39.6 B.

Against the composition that does the same rate work, the door is now 35.7% faster for admitted peers, 31.5% for random peers, and 54.5% for peers near the bans (estimated from 0.9's idle ratios), with 4.3 times its throughput at 32 threads. That meets NFR-10 as James decided it (D-125).

## Persistence rollback

Pass a `VersionAnchor` to `BouncerBuilder::version_anchor`. bouncer then records the store's version (its snapshot's epoch and the journal batches since) outside the store after every durable write. A store whose version is lower is refused at load with `PersistError::RolledBack` and `persist.rolled_back`. That covers an older backup put back, a journal cut after a whole batch, or a store that has gone missing. Without the check, such a store would bring back lifted bans and lose newer ones. `FileAnchor` keeps the version in one small file. Keep it where a restore of the store does not reach. Without an anchor, nothing changes.

## A false revocation under load

With every core busy, a revoking thread could be preempted inside its few-instruction slot write. A reader whose probe crossed that slot gave up after about 4 microseconds and failed closed. For a revocation check, failing closed revokes the whole connection, so a request nobody had revoked was stopped and its connection closed. The stress test caught it in 2 of 40 runs on 0.9.1. Readers now wait for a writer that holds a slot to run again, yielding and then sleeping so the writer gets the core, for up to one second before failing closed. A scheduler quantum was not enough: at a load of 500 on 32 cores a thread can wait well past 20 ms. Nothing changes while no writer holds the slot.

## Verification

- **Loom models with every side spawned.** In a model whose main thread acts right after spawning, loom never runs the spawned thread first. Eight models had that shape and now spawn every participant. All pass, and the two new models (L-11, for the filter) fail when their protection is removed.
- Two tests timed by wall clock are now bounded by what they check: one by a ratio within the run, one by a count of rounds on each side. A busy machine had pushed both past their limits.

## Upgrading

No change is needed. New:
- `VersionAnchor`, `StoreVersion`, and `FileAnchor`;
- `BouncerBuilder::version_anchor`;
- `PersistError::RolledBack`;
- `signals::PERSIST_ROLLED_BACK`.

`PersistError` and the signal list are non-exhaustive, so neither addition breaks a match. Ban references are now computed from the id alone; they never matched across seeds anyway.
